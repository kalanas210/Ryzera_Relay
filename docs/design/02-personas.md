## 1. Nuwan Perera, Dispatcher

**44 · Senior Dispatcher · Peliyagoda planning office · 14 years at Waypoint, started as a driver**

> "Everything is in my spreadsheet and in my head. When I'm on leave, the plan suffers."

**Profile.** Desk: two large monitors, a stable connection. Tools today: his spreadsheet, phone calls and WhatsApp. Hours: from 2:30 PM, and on call on Fresh nights.

**Context.** Nuwan works at two large monitors with a stable office connection. He is fast in Excel and knows every outlet's quirks from years on the road: which ones are van-only, which malls close their bays at 7 AM. After the 4 PM cutoff he plans tomorrow's runs for both depots. During the morning runs he fields calls from drivers and stores.

**A typical day**
- **2:30 PM** Starts his shift and chases stores that haven't ordered yet.
- **4:00 PM** Orders close. He re-types phone and WhatsApp orders into the spreadsheet.
- **4:30 to 7:00 PM** Matches orders to vehicles, checks refrigeration and access from memory, and decides what to defer.
- **Fresh nights** On call by phone, so the dock can reach him about a shortfall before the trucks leave.
- **Next morning** Learns about problems when a store calls to say the truck hasn't come.

**Goals**
- Build a feasible plan quickly after the cutoff.
- Hear about problems before the store does.
- Defend every deferral with facts, not memory.

**Pain points today**
- Orders arrive by phone and message, and he re-keys all of them.
- Refrigerated capacity (16 vehicles) is the bottleneck on busy Fresh days.
- Once vehicles leave, he is blind until someone calls.
- Deferrals are handwritten, so the same outlet can be skipped two runs in a row.

**What he needs from Relay**
- One queue of confirmed orders the moment orders close at 4 PM.
- A planning board that proposes an allocation and flags any broken constraint as he adjusts it.
- A reason for every deferral, split into what was unavoidable and what was a choice.
- A live view of stop events, and a weekly capacity view before paydays and festivals.

**Design implications**
- A dense, multi-panel desktop layout that works with the keyboard.
- Live meters for weight, volume, Fresh minutes and fuel; Planned and Expected times on every stop.
- A deferral can't be saved without a reason, and a store that waited last time is protected unless he overrides it.

## 2. Mohamed Rizwan, Loader

**31 · Loading Supervisor · Kandy hub dock · night shift**

> "The printed list said Hemmathagama goes in first. The plan changed after 9 PM and nobody told the dock."

**Profile.** Device: one shared tablet, mounted near the bays. Conditions: gloves, noise, dim light, a cold room. Language: Tamil and Sinhala; reads English slowly.

**Context.** Rizwan leads the night shift on the Kandy hub dock with Suresh Kumar and Anjali Wickramasinghe. The first Fresh runs leave at 2:00 AM and the last second trips after 6 AM, so each load has to be ready, in unloading order, before its vehicle leaves. The three of them share one tablet mounted near the bays. They wear gloves, the dock is noisy and dimly lit, and chilled goods come from a cold room. Rizwan speaks Tamil and Sinhala and reads English labels, though slowly.

The quote is the story's night seen without Relay: at 9:12 PM Nuwan swapped Kasun's stops 3 and 4, so Aranayake, not Hemmathagama, now loads first on VEH045 (reverse stop order). In Relay the loader sees it as the banner "Plan changed 9:12 PM".

**A typical night**
- **After 9 PM** Plans can still change, but the printed loading lists on the dock don't.
- **From 2:00 AM** Loads vehicle by vehicle as the runs leave, chilled goods only for the refrigerated vehicles.
- **At each handover** If anything is missing, he tells the driver out loud and hopes the message reaches the store.

**Goals**
- Load every vehicle right the first time, in unloading order.
- Catch missing or damaged goods before the vehicle leaves.
- Hand over on time with a clear record of what was loaded.

**Pain points today**
- Printed lists go stale when the plan changes.
- The stop order isn't on the list, so goods for the first stop end up at the back of the truck.
- Shortfalls are discovered at the outlet, not at the dock.
- There is no record of what was actually loaded.

**What he needs from Relay**
- A loading list for each vehicle and trip that follows every plan change.
- Items grouped by stop in reverse order, so the last stop is loaded first.
- Tap to check an item, and one tap to flag it missing or damaged. The flag reaches the dispatcher before departure.
- A clear "load complete" handover to the driver.

**Design implications**
- Large touch targets that work with gloves, and high contrast for a dim dock.
- Almost no typing.
- A quick PIN switch between loaders on the shared tablet.
- A language choice: Sinhala, Tamil, or English.
- Chilled items look visually distinct.

## 3. Kasun Bandara, Driver

**35 · Dry-box truck driver · Kandy hub · the Kegalle Fresh dry run on the Kandy corridor**

> "If a store says the rice never came, it's my word against theirs."

**Profile.** Truck: VEH045, a dry-box truck, 4,200 kg. Phone: own mid-range Android on prepaid data. Signal: comes and goes on the Kegalle hill roads. Language: Sinhala.

**Context.** Kasun drives VEH045 from the Kandy hub to four Fresh stores in Kegalle district: west on the A1 to Kegalle town, back to Mawanella, then the hill roads to Hemmathagama and Aranayake, where mobile coverage comes and goes. He uses his own mid-range Android phone on a prepaid data plan, mounted on the dashboard, and handles it only when stopped. He is most comfortable in Sinhala. Today he carries a paper run sheet, and route changes reach him by phone call.

VEH045 ran this Kegalle Fresh dry run on 94 of 115 Wednesdays, January 2024 to March 2026 (route history).

**A typical morning**
- **3:40 AM** Leaves the Kandy hub with the paper run sheet.
- **Until 7:30 AM** Four stores, each with its own window. Aranayake, the last, closes at 7:30.
- **Between stops** Gets calls about changes while driving and has to pull over to listen.
- **Later** A store disputes a delivery, and there is no proof.

**Goals**
- Reach every store inside its window.
- Get proof of delivery quickly and move on.
- No calls or phone use while driving.

**Pain points today**
- A paper run sheet whose planned times he is nearly always behind in monsoon months (98.9% of 616 first stops of Kegalle Fresh runs in monsoon months arrived after their planned time, route history).
- Changes arrive by phone while he is on the road.
- No proof of delivery, so disputes depend on memory.
- No signal on parts of the route, so an app that needs the internet fails him exactly where he is.

**What he needs from Relay**
- Today's run in stop order, with each store's window and access.
- Planned and Expected times, so behind plan isn't read as late.
- Arrival, delivery and proof (name plus photo or signature) in a few taps.
- Works offline and syncs by itself when the signal returns.
- One tap to report a delay or a problem, when stopped.

**Design implications**
- One-thumb, glanceable screens with big buttons, and one task per screen.
- Offline-first, with sync status always visible.
- Low data use (compressed photos) because he pays for his own data.
- Battery-friendly: no background tracking. A location stamp is taken only at stop events.
- Sinhala interface.

## 4. Dilani Jayawardena, Store Manager

**39 · Store Manager · Waypoint Fresh Hemmathagama, Kegalle district · deliveries 4:00 to 7:45 AM**

> "I order on WhatsApp and then I just hope. If the chilled order isn't coming, I need to know before I roster staff."

**Profile.** Devices: desktop in the office, phone on the floor. Orders: dry every operating day; chilled Wednesday to Saturday. Language: Sinhala and English.

**Context.** Dilani runs Waypoint Fresh Hemmathagama (OUT117), on the hill roads south of Mawanella, from a desktop PC in the back office and from her phone on the shop floor. She orders dry goods for every operating day and chilled goods for Wednesday to Saturday, so those mornings bring two orders, both through the rear dock. She works comfortably in Sinhala and English.

**A typical day**
- **Before 4 PM** Sends tomorrow's orders by phone or message, with no confirmation back.
- **From 4:00 AM** Has staff ready at the rear dock without knowing when the truck will come.
- **8:00 AM** Opens the store. If the chilled order didn't arrive, the dairy shelves stay empty.

**Goals**
- Get the right stock before opening.
- Have staff ready when the truck arrives.
- Resolve shortages and damage quickly.

**Pain points today**
- No confirmation that an order was received or scheduled.
- No expected arrival time.
- She finds out about deferrals only when the truck doesn't come.
- Disputes about what arrived rely on memory.

**What she needs from Relay**
- Place dry and chilled orders before 4 PM and get an instant confirmation.
- See each order's status and expected arrival time.
- A clear deferral notice with the reason and the new delivery date.
- Confirm receipt quickly, even before the driver's proof arrives, and report issues with photos.

**Design implications**
- A simple interface that works on both desktop and phone.
- Plain status words she never has to decode: *Received by Waypoint*, *Scheduled*, *On the way*, *Receipt confirmed*, or *Moved to Thursday*.
- "Everything arrived" for a clean delivery, with exceptions flagged line by line.

**We also design for:** Style mall stores, which accept deliveries only inside the mall's fixed window, and Tech stores, where high-value single items need a careful receipt check.

## How the four connect

Each handoff that breaks today, by voice or on paper, becomes a shared record in Relay (board 10):

1. **Places an order.** When **Dilani** sends her dry and chilled orders on Tuesday afternoon, they land in **Nuwan's** queue and she sees "Received by Waypoint" straight away.
2. **Publishes the plan.** When **Nuwan** publishes, **Rizwan's** loading lists follow every change, and Dilani hears that evening why her chilled order waits for Thursday.
3. **Hands over the load.** When **Rizwan** flags 6 rice and dhal cases missing, Nuwan decides from his phone before the truck leaves, so **Kasun's** load matches his run sheet.
4. **Delivers with proof.** When Kasun delivers with no signal, the record waits on his phone. Dilani can confirm receipt herself, and his proof attaches as soon as the phone syncs.
